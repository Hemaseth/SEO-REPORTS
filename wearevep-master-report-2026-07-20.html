    1. A linked service property is set to noindex — high impact if that was not intended

    The primary navigation on every page of the main site carries a Brand Activations item. It opens brandactivations.wearevep.com in a new tab — a live, separately-hosted property (Wix; server Pepyaka) selling Bay Area photo booth rentals and sales. That property returns HTTP 200 and serves this in its <head>:

    CheckResultConsequence
    Robots meta tagcontent="noindex"Excluded from all search indexes
    Served to Googlebot UAIdentical noindexNot a cloaking artefact
    Consistency3 of 3 spaced requestsNot transient
    Present in any sitemapNo — 0 references across all 246 URLsNo discovery path
    H1 elements0No heading signal
    Structured dataWebSite onlyNo LocalBusiness, Service, Product or Offer
    Body copy201 wordsBelow any competitive threshold

    The effect is unambiguous: this property cannot appear in organic search at all. Any referral or link pointing at photo booth rentals arrives at a page search engines are explicitly instructed to discard.

    What is not established is whether that is a mistake. A noindex flag is a deliberate switch, and there are legitimate reasons to set one — a property used only as a paid-campaign landing page, a line being retired, or a build still in progress. It is equally often a staging flag nobody switched off. From outside the business, those cases are indistinguishable.

    So this is listed first not because it is confirmed to be the worst problem, but because it has the widest gap between the two possible answers: if intentional, it is a non-issue; if not, it is the most expensive item in this report, because it costs a whole channel rather than a few positions. It is placed at the top of the roadmap as a question to answer, not a defect to fix. One conversation settles it.

    It is deliberately not rated critical. Every other finding here was independently re-measured and required to survive an attempt to refute it before being reported, and unconfirmed intent does not clear that bar. Rating it critical would mean presenting an inference as a measurement.

    4. HTML is effectively not being edge-cached — 59 of 60 requests missed cache

    Home-page TTFB measured 1,886 ms in a Chrome trace, which is 82% of that page's entire LCP time. The obvious conclusion — a slow server — is wrong, and worth showing because it changes the fix completely.

    The same trace on /audio-visual/ returned TTFB of 27 ms. The difference was CDN cache state. So the site was measured across 30 URLs, twice each:

    PassCache stateRequestsMedian TTFB
    1MISS30 / 302,591 ms
    2 (immediate repeat)MISS29 / 302,651 ms
    2HIT1 / 3082 ms

    Requesting the same URL twice in a row still missed cache. The server is not slow — when a page is served from cache it responds in 27–82 ms. The site is simply not retaining cached HTML, leaving roughly 2.5 seconds of avoidable latency on nearly every first visit, sitewide. This is the highest-leverage performance item in the report, and it is a configuration question rather than a code change.

    ● Medium · Local Search · Data Integrity

    5. The business address on the site conflicts with its own directory listings

    The site's LocalBusiness structured data and page content give a San Leandro address. Yelp (348 reviews), WeddingWire (187 reviews) and two further directories list Berkeley. The phone number is identical across all of them, which confirms these describe one business rather than two.

    Address consistency across citations is a direct input to local pack ranking, and this business has meaningful review equity — over 500 reviews — attached to the conflicting record. Separately, the real address appears only inside JSON-LD: there is no visible address in the page copy on the contact page, and no embedded map.

    Which address is correct cannot be determined from outside. This needs a client answer before anything is changed.

    Direction — Confirm the current trading address, then align the site, the structured data and every third-party citation to it. Auditing the full citation set and sequencing corrections is part of the engagement scope.
    ● Medium · Content Architecture · Discovery

    6. The two largest pages on the site have no internal links pointing to them

    /your-vision-amplified-capabilities-guide/ (2,604 words — the longest page on the site) and /vep-difference/ (1,688 words — fifth longest) receive zero inbound links from any of the other 113 pages. Not from navigation, not from the footer, not from body copy. Both are indexable with valid canonicals, so they are reachable only by a crawler reading the XML sitemap.

    For comparison, every page in the main navigation carries 113 inbound page-level references. These two are differentiator and capability content — exactly the material a considering buyer should encounter — and internally they are invisible.

    Direction — Give both pages a genuine route in from the pages whose readers they are meant to persuade. Deciding where they belong in the site's information architecture is part of the engagement scope.
    ● Medium · Content Architecture · Internal Linking

    7. Most blog posts route readers to the contact form rather than the service they describe

    19 of 33 blog posts link only to /contact-us/ in their body copy, never to the service page covering the topic they just explained. A post about uplighting does not link to the lighting service; a post about choosing a DJ does not link to DJ entertainment.

    The remaining 14 posts do link correctly, which establishes this is an editorial inconsistency rather than a template constraint. The cost is on both sides: service pages lose the topical internal links that help them rank, and readers who are ready to act get sent to a generic form instead of the relevant page.

    Direction — Route each post to the service page it supports, using anchor text that matches the topic. Mapping posts to targets across the archive is part of the engagement scope.
    ● Medium · Performance · Image Delivery

    8. A 63-image logo gallery is forced to load eagerly on four pages, two of them money pages

    The client-logo gallery carries no-lazyload skip-lazy classes that deliberately override the site's own lazy-loading system. All 63 images load immediately on the home page, corporate events, lighting and audio visual — roughly 1.2 MB, the majority of eager image weight on those pages, for a strip most visitors scroll past.

    Kept at medium rather than raised: measured lab LCP on these pages still passes, so this is not currently breaking a threshold. It is avoidable weight that compounds the caching problem above, particularly on mobile.

    Direction — Restore the site's own lazy-loading behaviour for below-the-fold galleries. Verifying no visual regression at each breakpoint is part of the engagement scope.
    ● Medium · Images · Format

    9. No modern image formats are served — confirmed by live content negotiation, not assumed

    Requests sent with an Accept header advertising WebP and AVIF support were answered with legacy JPEG and PNG. 1,017 of 1,022 raster images (99.5%) are legacy formats, and no modern-format sibling files exist to negotiate to.

    WebP typically reduces image payload 25–35% at equivalent quality. On an image-led portfolio site this is the largest single lever on page weight after the caching issue.

    Direction — Introduce modern-format delivery with correct fallbacks, sequenced after the caching work since both address the same page-weight problem. Selecting the conversion approach for this stack is part of the engagement scope.

    Claims tested and rejected

    Every candidate finding was independently re-measured before it entered this report, and anything that could not be re-confirmed was dropped. Of 33 candidates, 8 were rejected. They are recorded here because what an audit declines to claim says as much as what it does.

    ClaimWhy it was rejected
    “AI crawlers are blocked”False. GPTBot and OAI-SearchBot initially returned 429, but re-testing with spaced requests returned 200 — the earlier failures were caused by our own request rate. Every search and answer-engine crawler reaches the site normally.
    “No llms.txt file”False. The file exists at /llms.txt, is a genuine Yoast-generated document, and is not a soft 404. Only its truncation is a real issue, recorded at low priority.
    “Server response time is 1.9 seconds”Misattributed. That reading was a cold cache miss. The same infrastructure returned 27 ms when serving from cache. The real defect is the cache miss rate, which has a different cause and a different fix.
    “28 orphaned pages”Count unsupported. The category is real but the figure did not survive re-measurement. Only 2 orphans re-confirmed cleanly; those are reported.
    “Zero review schema is a high-severity defect”Fact true, severity wrong. The markup is genuinely absent, but it neither blocks indexing nor suppresses a money page, so it does not meet the bar for high.
    “Five money pages are under-linked”The raw link counts reproduced, but the definition of which pages qualified as money pages did not hold up on review.
    “Missing service-area landing pages”The absence is real, but nothing established that it is suppressing anything measurable. Recorded as an idea, not a defect.
    “Absent from search for a commercial query”Not reproducible. A second independent search returned a different top ten, so the result is not stable enough to report as measured.

    Two of these — the AI crawler block and the slow server — would have been the most alarming findings in the report. Both were wrong.

    Instrument before judging

    Six things would materially raise the confidence of the next audit. Listed by value.

    AdditionWhat it would resolveValue
    Search Console accessConverts every ranking statement from a snapshot into a trend, and settles the archive dilution question with impression data instead of inference. Also surfaces coverage errors invisible from outside.Highest
    Analytics accessTurns conversion findings from structure into behaviour — whether the quote form actually loses people, and whether empty portfolio pages fail to convert or simply are not reached.Highest
    Rank trackingBoth competitive findings rest on single searches. Tracked positions would establish whether the wedding DJ gap is real, seasonal, or measurement noise.High
    Backlink dataThe one dimension entirely absent here. Without it, an on-page gap and an authority gap look identical — and the recommended fix differs completely.High

    Two structural notes on method. Every finding here was re-measured by an independent reviewer instructed to refute it, and anything that could not be re-confirmed was dropped — that is why 8 of 33 candidates are absent. And the crawl was taken fresh on the audit date; no earlier data was carried forward, because search conditions and the site both change faster than a report's shelf life.
