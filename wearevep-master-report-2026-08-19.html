Summary

The short version

Three findings carry this audit. Read these and you have it.

1 · The first AI-answer win in this engagement's history — on one query

Google's AI Overview for "best event production companies san francisco" now names Verducci Event Productions and describes it accurately, grouped with EventWorks AV and Owl Vision under audio-visual and technical production. Across the May and July baselines the brand appeared in no AI answer at all. It is real and it is narrow: on the two other queries that returned an AI Overview, the brand is absent — including wedding DJ, where the answer names four rivals with ratings and neighbourhoods attached.

2 · The most quotable page on the site has no internal links pointing at it

/faqs/ carries 2,148 words, 56 genuine question-and-answer pairs in valid FAQ markup, and seven real prices between $150 and $2,000. That is precisely the shape answer engines retrieve and quote. Every link to it comes from the shared navigation; not one page links to it from its body copy. Meanwhile The Knot publishes a $3,000 starting price for the business that the business never states itself.

Direction — Route internal links into the FAQ asset and lift its pricing answers onto the commercial pages they belong to. Specification, sequencing and build sit within engagement scope.

3 · The site tells machines the wrong identity

The homepage markup names an Instagram handle and a LinkedIn profile that are not the accounts ranking for the brand. Two separate Yelp listings exist for one business. Review sites call it "Verducci Entertainment" as often as "Verducci Event Productions". An engine resolving this entity has to decide whether these are one company or several — and nothing on the site settles it.

Direction — Consolidate the identity graph so every asserted profile is the live one, and assert the review and pricing evidence the business already owns. Specification, sequencing and build sit within engagement scope.

The 403s prove nothing — they are the standard response to any request claiming to be a known crawler from an unverified address, and Google indexes the site perfectly well. The 429s are the finding: a separate status, returned only to the AI-training and data-licensing cohort. That is a host-level policy rather than a site misconfiguration, but the consequence lands here.

The stated policy and the enforced policy disagree. robots.txt is three lines and says Allow: /. Nothing in it discloses that a class of crawlers is being turned away.
Direction — Decide the AI-crawler position deliberately, then make robots.txt state it honestly and take the platform question up with the host. Specification, sequencing and build sit within engagement scope.

Sequence

What to do first

Ordered by the ratio of measured impact to effort. Direction only — specification and build are engagement scope.

1 · Settle the identity

Every asserted profile pointed at the live account, the duplicate directory listing resolved, the organisation facts the branded prompts ask for actually present. Nothing else in AI visibility compounds until the engines agree who this company is.

2 · Surface the answers already written

56 answers and seven real prices exist. Route the site's own links into them, and assert the reviews and pricing as machine-readable claims rather than prose.

3 · Decide and disclose the AI-crawler position

A class of crawlers is being turned away while robots.txt says everything is allowed. Whichever way that decision goes, the file should state it.

4 · Attach geography to existing proof

19 portfolio entries, zero place names, hub set to noindex. The rivals gaining geographic relevance are doing it with work they had already done.

5 · Widen the ranking surface past two URLs

And pursue placement in the lists that own the category queries, since those are what the AI answers are reading.

6 · Restructure commercial pages around buying questions

Fifteen pages carry a question-shaped heading. The twelve worst-served prompts are all buying-stage.

7 · Lift the retrievability ceiling

Caching and image delivery, both already root-caused.

Each item above is a direction, not a specification. Turning any of them into a build — the exact markup, the page structures, the link routing, the outreach targets — is engagement scope, and is what the implementation phase covers.

Limits

What this audit could not measure

Named rather than estimated, in line with the evidence standard governing every report in this engagement.

The four-engine citation baseline did not complete. Sixty tracked prompts were commissioned across Google AI Overview, Google AI Mode, ChatGPT Search and Perplexity. The data provider's account reached its monthly usage limit mid-run; three batches aborted after 11, 9 and 9 of 20 prompts, and the partial results were locked before they could be read. Nothing in this report is inferred from that missing data.

Consequently unmeasured: citation rate across the four engines; whether the engines state the location, service list and ownership correctly; whether published FAQ pricing is being retrieved on pricing prompts; which competitor set the models believe exists; and whether the 429 gate measurably suppresses citations.

Also unmeasured: Google Business Profile completeness, which needs owner access — no profile panel surfaced in the captures. Backlink and anchor-text profile, which needs a tool outside this engagement. Core Web Vitals field data; the speed figures here are lab readings and the performance position is held at 55* pending real field measurement.

The eight-query capture of 18 August is a genuine reading but it is one engine surface. It should not be treated as the baseline. Restoring provider credit and re-running the tracked set is the first action of the next phase.
